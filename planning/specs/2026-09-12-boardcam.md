# BoardCam — clock-triggered OTB game recorder (photos → PGN)

## Problem
Isaac and a friend play over-the-board rapid (10+5) with a phone chess clock
and want to analyse every game afterwards, but neither remembers the full move
sequence, so recreating the game in an engine is slow and partial. Photographing
key positions by hand interrupts play, and single-photo LLM transcription of
this abstract-piece set has proven unreliable (flipped colours, confused piece
types, wrong squares). What is needed is a zero-workload recorder: a second
phone on the existing stand photographs the board every time the clock is
pressed, and the game comes out the other end as a correct PGN.

## Solution
Two phones open one web app served from Isaac's Mac through an ngrok tunnel
(HTTPS is mandatory for mobile camera access). The **clock phone** runs the
chess clock; every press is an event over WebSocket. The **camera phone** sits on
the stand, holds a live camera stream, and on each press grabs a short burst of
frames and uploads them. Frame 0 (start position) is captured automatically
before the first press.

The engine never classifies piece types from pixels. Because the start position
is known and every ply is photographed, the position is *tracked*: each new
frame is compared to the previous one, the pattern of changed squares plus a
per-square occupancy cue is scored against every legal move (also "no move" and
two-move pairs, to survive a missed or double press), and a beam search over
the whole game picks the globally most likely sequence — later frames resolve
earlier ambiguities. Board corners, square colours and which side is white are
detected automatically per game (camera position, lighting, board and pieces
may all change between games). The camera sits low and to the side (~30°
elevation), so pieces hide the squares behind them; the engine therefore reads
moves from where change blobs *touch the board* (piece footprints projected
through the calibration), and analyses only the board area plus a strip above
its far edge, so hands, players and captured pieces beside the board are
invisible to it. Low-confidence plies are resolved automatically
by a vision-LLM call (Sonnet 5, escalate to Opus 5) that chooses among the
engine's candidates. Output: PGN with `%clk` times, one-tap lichess import, and
a review page where a wrong ply can be corrected; corrections re-run the tracker
from that ply and become labelled data for a tuning script that refits the
engine's scoring parameters. The engine is built and gated entirely on a
synthetic renderer that randomises camera pose, lighting, board/piece styles,
orientation, hands and jitter; the first real games are the final phase.

## Notes

### A. Protocol (Phase 1 writes the authoritative `docs/PROTOCOL.md`; this is the design)
- Room: camera phone creates the game (`POST /games`) and shows a 4-char code + QR; clock phone joins with the code. Both connect to `WS /ws/{room}?role=`. Rejoin with the same role replaces the stale socket. Heartbeat every 10 s.
- Clock → server events (persisted, forwarded): `clock.config{initial_ms, increment_ms, white_name, black_name}`, `clock.start{t}` (white's first press, seq 1), `clock.press{seq, side, white_ms, black_ms, t}`, `clock.pause`, `clock.resume`, `clock.flag{side}`, `clock.stop{result}`, `clock.reset`.
- Server → camera: `capture{seq}` on start/press/flag/stop (stop captures one final frame in case the last move was never pressed). Camera uploads `POST /games/{id}/frames/{seq}/{k}` for k in 0..2 (burst at +0/+400/+900 ms; cut short if a new `capture` arrives). Pre-start: camera uploads frame 0 every 2 s until `clock.start`.
- Server → clock: `frame.ok{seq, k}`, `calibration{ok, score, warning}`, `analysis.ready{pgn, lichess_url, flagged}`.
- Layout: `data/games/<id>/{events.jsonl, frames/<seq>_<k>.jpg, calibration.json, analysis.json, game.pgn, labels.json}`.
- Seq n corresponds to ply n in the normal case; the tracker never assumes it (0/1/2 plies per frame).

### B. Calibration method (Phase 2)
1. Downscale to 1024 long edge, grey, CLAHE, Canny, `HoughLinesP`. Cluster lines by angle into two families (the board's two edge directions; they need not be perpendicular in the image).
2. In each family, find groups of ≥ 7 roughly evenly spaced lines (a perspective-consistent progression) — that is the grid. Take the outermost lines of each group; intersect → 4 corner candidates. Sub-pixel refine with `cornerSubPix`.
3. Validate: rectify to 512², compute the checker score = normalised correlation between the rectified ranks 3–6 (empty at frame 0) and the ideal ± checker pattern for the two possible colour phases; take the better phase; require score ≥ `checker_min`. Also require the two occupied bands (ranks 1–2 and 7–8) to differ from their squares' background far more than ranks 3–6 do.
4. Fallbacks in order: next-best line group; vision-LLM asked for approximate normalised corner coordinates then local line search around them (Phase 4 wires the LLM; Phase 2 leaves the hook); manual corner drag on the camera phone.
5. Camera side = board edge whose image-space length is largest (nearest the camera). Square colours from step 3's phase. White side = occupied band whose piece pixels are brighter (piece pixels = pixels far from the square's own empty-colour model; compare medians). Handedness: with white side and square colours known, choose the unique rotation where white's right-hand corner square is light.
6. Per-frame drift: re-run steps 1–3 restricted to a ±20 px band around the previous corners; accept if the checker score holds, else keep the prior corners and emit `drift`.

### C. Emission model (Phase 3 — implement as written, then tune)
Geometry that drives everything here: the camera is ~30° above the board plane
and to the side. A piece of height h standing on square S projects a silhouette
that extends `h / tan(elev)` (≈ 1.7 h at 30°) *away from the camera* beyond S in
the rectified board plane — up to three squares for a king. So the pixels of a
square mostly show whatever stands in front of it, and per-square patch
statistics are unreliable. What *is* reliable is where a silhouette touches the
board: the lowest image-space edge of a piece's change region is its base, and
projecting that through the calibration lands on the piece's true square.

**Mask.** Only pixels inside the board quadrilateral plus a strip above its far
edge (height `max_piece_h · f / tan(elev)`, estimated from the calibration with
an approximate focal length = image width) are analysed. Hands at rest, players,
the clock phone and captured pieces beside the board are outside the mask.

**Change blobs.** `R_t` is the masked raw frame (median of surviving burst
candidates) photometrically normalised to `R_{t−1}` inside the mask. Change mask
`M = |grey(R_t) − grey(R_{t−1})| > θ`, morphological open then close (3 px),
connected components, drop blobs with area < `min_area`. Blobs cut by the
mask boundary at the *far* strip (torso/background motion) with no pixels
reaching the board interior are discarded.

**Footprints.** For each blob b: footprint pixels = the lowest 20 % of b along
the image-space vertical (toward the camera side); project them through `H⁻¹`
to board coordinates; `mass_b(s)` = fraction of footprint pixels landing in
square s, then spread **ray-consistently**: a partially hidden piece's visible
bottom lies *beyond* its true square along the camera ray, so `mass_b` is also
credited, with geometric decay `ρ^k` (ρ = 0.4), to the k-th square nearer the
camera along the projection direction. `z_s = Σ_b mass_b(s)`; `A_b` = blob
area, capped at `cap_area` so one hand or torso cannot dominate.

**Occupancy evidence** (secondary). Square s is *observable* at frame t iff
the tracked state has no piece on any of the squares within `ceil(h_type /
(w·tan(elev)))` squares directly in front of s toward the camera. For
observable squares only: empty-colour model `(μ_s, Σ_s)` in Lab over the near
strip of s (the 30 % of the square nearest the camera, which its own piece's
base covers), bootstrapped at frame 0 from observable empty squares of the same
colour and updated by exponential averaging while s is tracked as empty;
`o_s` = Mahalanobis distance; `p(occupied | o_s) = σ(a·(o_s − b))`.

**Candidates** for the transition t−1 → t from board B: `∅` (nothing changed),
each legal move m, and pairs (m₁, m₂) with m₂ legal after m₁ — pairs are only
generated when the best single candidate's margin over `∅` and the runner-up is
below `pair_trigger`. Promotions are 4 candidates each.

**Changed set** `C(c)`: squares whose content differs between B and B·c
(from, to, rook squares for castling, captured pawn for e.p.).

**Log-likelihood.**
```
L(c) = Σ_{s∈C(c)}  log( ε_hit + (1−ε_hit) · min(1, z_s / m_hit) )        # each changed square must be hit by footprint mass
     − λ_unexpl · Σ_b  min(A_b, cap_area) · (1 − Σ_{s∈C(c)} mass_b(s))   # blob mass not explained by the candidate
     + Σ_{s observable} log p(occ_s = occupied_after(B·c, s) | o_s)      # secondary, only where the view is clear
     + log prior(c)
```
with `prior(∅) = 0.02`, `prior(m) = 0.95 / |legal|`, `prior(pair) = 0.03 /
|pairs|`. Initial params: `θ = 25 (grey levels), min_area = 40 px, m_hit = 0.15,
ε_hit = 0.03, λ_unexpl = 2.5 (per 1000 px), cap_area = 4000 px, ρ = 0.4,
a = 2, b = 3, pair_trigger = 2.0 nats, divergence_floor = −60, τ = 3.0 nats`.
All live in `engine/params.json`; `scripts/tune.py` refits them. A move whose
from- and to-squares are both fully hidden behind taller pieces (possible at 30°)
yields little footprint mass; that is exactly the case the beam, the occupancy
term on the *revealed* squares, and the Phase 4 LLM fallback exist for.

**Beam.** Width 5 over full game paths. Per-ply posterior margin = log-sum-exp difference between paths agreeing and disagreeing with the best path at that ply. Margin < τ → `low_margin` flag (→ LLM in Phase 4). Best `L` < `divergence_floor` → `unexplained`. Promotion variants that later moves cannot distinguish → `promotion_unknown` (default queen, LLM asked). Frame 1 must change squares on white's side; if instead black's side changed, flip `white_side` and restart — the second orientation check.

**Why this works without piece recognition.** Identity is carried by the tracked state; the image only has to answer "where did change touch the board" and, where the view is clear, "is this square occupied" — both near-binary signals with a fixed camera. Legal-move constraints turn a noisy 64-vector into a choice among ~30 discrete hypotheses, and the beam lets later frames vote on earlier ones.

### D. Decisions taken in this spec (from Isaac's answers, 2026-09-12)
- Hosting: Mac + ngrok (static free domain) — the Mac must be at the venue or reachable. Cloudflare Tunnel is the documented alternative if the ngrok interstitial becomes annoying (needs a domain).
- Fully automatic first; review page is for *correcting* not confirming, and every correction is training data for `tune.py`. "Training" here is refitting the scoring parameters and growing the labelled corpus; a learned piece classifier is deliberately out of scope.
- Synthetic-first because camera position, lighting, board, pieces and white's side all vary between games — so per-game auto-calibration is a hard requirement, not a nicety.
- Camera geometry confirmed 2026-09-12: ~2 ft above and 3–4 ft to the side (~30° elevation), players' hands, bodies and captured pieces in frame. The `shallow` renderer profile reproduces this and is the primary gate; the emission model is footprint-based for that reason.
- Stack: Python 3.13 venv (opencv wheels; 3.14 not guaranteed), FastAPI, python-chess, OpenCV headless, vanilla JS PWA (no build step), kaczor-design tokens.
- Out of scope for V1: takebacks, adjustments outside a move (j'adoube) — handled by correction; chess.com import (no API; copy PGN); multi-game sessions without reset; Bluetooth pairing (Web Bluetooth is absent on iOS Safari).

### E. Risks
- Synthetic-to-real gap in calibration (real boards have coordinates, borders, glare). Mitigation: Phase 6 tuning + manual-corner fallback that never blocks a game.
- Hand in every burst candidate during fast time-trouble play. Mitigation: bursts are cheap; the tracker tolerates a bad frame by scoring it as `∅`/`unexplained` and catching up with a pair on the next frame.
- Shallow camera (~30°): pieces hide up to three squares behind them, and a moved piece can be almost fully hidden. Mitigation: footprint-based scoring with ray-consistent spread, occupancy only on observable squares, `shallow` is the primary synthetic gate, LLM fallback; guidance to mount the phone as high as the stand allows because every 10° of elevation shrinks occlusion.
- Players, hands and captured pieces moving in frame. Mitigation: hard mask (board + far strip), blob-mass cap, far-strip blobs that never reach the board interior are discarded, burst filtering; the renderer's `shallow`/`hard` profiles include all of this noise so the gate measures it.
- iOS Safari pausing the camera stream when the screen dims. Mitigation: wake lock; documented "keep the camera page in the foreground".

### 2026-09-12 — Phase 1 execution deviations

**Agents: no worktree isolation.** The spec called for the two page agents to
run with `isolation: "worktree"`. They ran in the main tree instead, because
the main agent wrote the two shared files (`static/js/ws.js`,
`static/css/boardcam.css`) up front, which left the agents' file sets disjoint
(`clock.*` vs `camera.*`). No worktree meant no merge step. This worked and is
the better default whenever the shared surface can be written first.

**The camera-page agent did not deliver.** Agent A (clock) finished in ~5
minutes with 18 passing tests. Agent B (camera) ran for over an hour without
writing either of its files to disk; it was sunk in verifying a from-scratch QR
encoder in its scratchpad. It was sent a message to ship the files and drop the
QR work, did not, and was stopped. The main agent wrote `static/camera.html`,
`static/js/camera.js` and `static/js/qr.js` directly.

*Lesson for later phases:* a subagent brief that includes a self-contained,
hard, verifiable sub-problem (here: implement and verify a QR encoder) invites
the agent to spend its whole budget there and deliver nothing. Either carve
that sub-problem out as its own agent with its own file to write, or tell the
agent to write files first and refine second. Phase 5's review-page agent has
no comparable sub-problem, so the plan stands.

**Extra files beyond the spec's list.** `static/js/qr.js` (QR encoder, split
out so it is testable under node), `static/js/clocksm.js` (the pure clock state
machine, split from `clock.js` so `node --test` can drive it without a DOM),

**Two defects found and fixed during validation, both worth knowing about:**
1. `clock.stop` and `clock.flag` were sent without a `seq`, so the server never
   told the camera to capture. The final position of every game would have gone

### 2026-09-12 — Phase 2 execution deviations

**Camera side restricted to the two file sides, in every profile.** The spec
said azimuth U(−35°,+35°) "around a random board side". Rendered that way, a
camera behind a seated player at 25–40° photographs the player, not the board —
the torso fills the frame and the board is a sliver behind it. Real setups
cannot do this either, and `docs/SETUP.md` already tells Isaac to mount the
phone on the free side perpendicular to the players. So `sides=(1, 3)` for all
profiles; the ±35° azimuth jitter stays, so the camera is often well off
perpendicular. The engine is therefore *not* gated on a camera behind a player,
by design rather than by omission.

**Piece heights doubled from the first draft.** The renderer initially had a
king 0.97 squares tall. Real proportions are a 55 mm square and a 95 mm king,
so 1.75 squares — which is exactly what makes a king occlude ~3 squares at 30°,
the number Notes §C sizes the whole emission model on. Fixed before any gate
was run. Do not "fix" a Phase 3 tracking problem by shrinking the pieces back.

**Two renderer bugs found by looking at the output, not by a test.** The
`clean` profile was drawing players and props because `_draw_players` lost its
`if not s.players: return` guard in an edit; the `clean` corpus was regenerated
after the fix. The profile flags now drive a single `Scene.props` field so the
phone and the captured-piece pile are gated with the players.

**Calibration needed a step the spec's Notes §B does not contain.** Notes §B
(Hough → line families → outermost lines with periodic interior → corners →
sub-pixel refine → checker validation) was implemented as written and reached
85 % on `shallow`, short of the 95 % gate. The residual failures were all the
same: at 30° the far edge runs ~38 px per square against ~80 px on the near
edge, the line fit lands one square out in the foreshortened direction, and
`cornerSubPix` then locks onto that shifted grid because a board shifted by one
file is still a checkerboard. Added a final **hill-climb over whole-square grid
offsets** (shift, re-refine, re-score, keep what improves), which takes
`shallow` from 85 % to 97.5 %. Three smaller departures from §B, all in
`engine/README.md`: the progression is fitted in `w = 1/(s − s_inf)` rather than
by affine rectification (no horizon to go singular); candidates are (anchor,
spacing) pairs scored by the longest *consecutive* run with one line per index,
because the border's outer edge otherwise drags the fit at exactly the two
indices that become corners; and the white-band luminance margin votes in
candidate selection, not just in the warnings.

**`recalibrate` redesigned.** The spec had it re-running detection in a ±20 px
band. It now re-snaps the prior corners onto the new frame's interior saddles
first, which is cheaper, keeps the orientation the start frame established, and
is what a bump actually calls for; the banded re-detection remains as the
fallback.

**Classic PGNs: 29 entered, 25 usable.** Games are validated at import and
truncated at the first move that does not parse; four fell below the 30-ply
floor and are dropped, seven are truncated prefixes of the real game. That
clears the spec's "≥ 20 bundled classic PGNs". The corpus is topped up with
weighted random playouts, and `build_corpus` puts two feature-targeted playouts
first so *any* corpus of any size contains castling, en passant, promotion and
underpromotion.

**Extra file beyond the spec's list.** `synth/profiles.py` was in the plan;
`synth/generate.py` also gained `--max-plies`, which truncates every game so the
tests can render a corpus in seconds instead of minutes. Calibration only ever
looks at frame 0, so a 2-ply corpus tests it fully.

**Gate result.** Pooled clean+shallow+hard: 97/100 start frames within 3 px
(gate: ≥ 95 %), orientation correct on 100 % of those (gate: 100 %). Per-profile
numbers are in `engine/README.md`. Corpus generation takes ~9 minutes on 9
workers and 1.5 GB on disk.

**`Resume with` file list for Phase 3 was right.** Phase 2 read `CLAUDE.md` and
`docs/PROTOCOL.md` and needed nothing else.
